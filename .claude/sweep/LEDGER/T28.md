# T28 — THE REPO'S OWN TESTS: are they alive, honest, and cleaning up after themselves?

Sweep #6, terminal 28's territory — **never filed by T28, swept by T30 on 2026-08-22** after the
owner asked for the remaining work. Phases **P13501–P14000** (500), permanent and never renumbered.

**Territory:** `scripts/**` (199 files, 184 `.mjs`, ~42,000 lines) · `tests/**` (3 files) · the
**141** `verify:*` / `test:*` entries in `package.json`.

**Why this territory mattered most.** A permanently-red or silently-dead guard hides real
regressions. `verify:cache` — the 3D no-re-fetch check CLAUDE.md tells everyone to run — sat green
and asserted **nothing** for a month, because its subject had deliberately stopped doing the thing
it waited for. Nobody noticed. This pass asks the same question of all 141.

**The pattern, and it is the finding that matters:** not one fault was a *dead* guard. Every one was

guards' inserts silently began to fail. **A guard that does not check its own write cannot tell "the
product is wrong" from "my fixture never landed" — and it reports the first.**

## What running the app-dependent half actually taught (added 2026-08-22, second pass)

The 39 guards that need a running app were run against a dev server on port 4130. **Every failure
was the guard's own assumption about the screen — not one was a product fault.** Three of the four
failed *silently*, which is what makes this class expensive:

| guard | what it assumed | what is actually true |
|---|---|---|
| `verify:live-rush` | the RPC's state word appears in the tile's VISIBLE text | `floorTileHtml()` gives row 3 to whichever is more informative: with dishes the visible text is the progress (`0/1 served`) and the label moves to the row's `title`; with no dishes the label IS the visible text. It could only ever pass on an empty table. |
| `verify:customers` | `printBill` opens a window it can stub and read | when a printer is routed to a helper computer (mig 341) the bill goes to the basket and **"no window opens here at all, which is the whole point"**. The capture was empty, so every assertion on it failed — reporting a paper fault that did not exist. |
| `verify:merged-floor` | the payment popup is up 2.5s after the click | `markTablePaid()` awaits `ensurePartySlices()` first — a real round-trip for a four-table party — and the popup grew a tip row. The method click was skipped by a bare `if (count)`, so **nothing was asked for**, and the failure landed 30s later reading exactly like the product refusing to settle a merged party's bill. |
| `verify:offline` | — | **nothing wrong.** It requires a production build and REFUSES a dev server in one sentence: *"This server cannot be tested for offline behaviour, so nothing below was run."* Its own header records that the old usage line pointed at a dev server and *"that is exactly how it kept happening"*. A model refusal. |

**The rule these three break is one this codebase has already written down twice.** The payment-ring
check inside `verify-manager-live-rush.mjs` says *"the tile's outline is the payment signal, so check
the CLASS, not the label"* — and its neighbour asserted on the label. The wording territory (T27)
states the same rule for the product: **assert on the code, never on the prose that happens to be
rendered.** All three fixes obey it: read the `title` and the state class; force the documented
window fallback rather than guessing which print route is configured; select the method button by
`data-method`, not by its text.

**And the sharper rule, which is new:** a conditional click — `if (await x.count()) await x.click()`
— is a silent no-op the moment the control is slow. In a guard that becomes a phantom product fault
thirty seconds downstream. **Wait for the control, then assert that it was there.**

## The verdict on the app-dependent half — 11 reds, 11 explained, 0 product faults

Every guard that needed a running app has now been run, and **every single failure was the guard,
not the product.** Three separate faults produced a failure that read like a money or ordering bug:

| the guard said | what was actually happening |
|---|---|
| *"the whole party is paid"* failing after mark-paid | the payment popup now does a round-trip first, so a bare `if (count)` skipped the method click. **Nothing was ever asked for.** |
| *"the parent's ticket really was voided — {27:received, 28:received}"* | the removal sheet grew a second question ("was the food made?") and Remove stays disabled until both are answered. The click did nothing. |
| *"a figure that was not its own reply's"* on the admin's money tile | a flat 8.5s sleep on a route that compiles on first hit — the tile still held its `…` placeholder and the reply had not landed. |

**All three were SILENT.** A conditional click, an unanswered question, a fixed sleep — each produced
a confident failure thirty seconds downstream that pointed at the app. That is the most expensive
kind of false alarm in this repo, because the thing it accuses is the money.

**The rule, now three for three:** when a guard here reports a money or ordering fault, check first
whether it actually managed to *ask*. Wait for the control, then assert it was there.

And the counterpart, from `verify:families`: **a guard can also fail on the BEST possible answer.**
It demanded 401/403 from a staff-only function and got 404 — because that function carries no anon
grant at all, so PostgREST cannot even see it for that role. A thing anon cannot find is stronger
evidence than a thing that politely declines.

## The four faults

1. **`verify:cancelled-tile` had been failing every run and calling it a "test setup issue".** Its
   cleanup was refused by the bill-immutability trigger (mig 190, widened by mig 361 from "a bill
   got a number" to "an order was paid or **served**") and the result was discarded, so its own
   leftover open session blocked the next run's insert forever. Now takes the compliant route the
   trigger names — close + `deleted_at` — and proves the table is clear. Its three fixture inserts
   also omitted newly-required columns and ignored their results. Green twice in a row, and it once
   again proves the bug it was written for.
2. **`verify:customers` flipped a real setting and restored an assumed value.** It sets
   `bill_customer_print` off, then restored a hard-coded `true` — turning the switch ON at a
   restaurant that had it off — with nothing running on a kill. Now restores what it found, on
   `SIGINT`, `SIGTERM` and a crash.
3. **A new duplicated migration number.** Two branches both took 352. The newer moved to **364**,
   after proving none of the 11 migrations above it touches any of its 5 objects.
4. **Two guards bound a FIXED port for their own stub server** — now on an ephemeral port.
   ⚠️ **This item was reported wrongly the first time and is corrected here.** The claim was "three
   guards default to port 4000, the owner's own window". They do not. All three
   (`verify:offline-retry`, `verify:warm-shell`, `verify:outbox`) **start their own tiny stub server**
   and never touch the app at all — the finding was a false inference from "no `--base` found". They
   run green, self-contained: 17 + 13 + 26 = 56 checks. The real, smaller fault behind it: two of
   them bound a **fixed** port (4327, 4324) while their sibling `verify-offline-retry.mjs` already
   used `listen(0, "127.0.0.1")` and read the port back. Two worktrees running either one at the same
   moment collide on `EADDRINUSE` and the guard fails for a reason that has nothing to do with the
   product. Both now use port 0. Nothing outside those files knows the number.
